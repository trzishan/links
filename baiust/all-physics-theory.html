Gauss's Law & Coulomb's Law Derivation

Gauss's Law Statement: The total electric flux ΦE through any closed Gaussian surface is equal to the net enclosed electric charge Qenc divided by the permittivity of free space ε0.

ΦE = ∮ E · dA =
Qenc ε0

Step-by-Step Derivation of Coulomb's Law from Gauss's Law:

  1. Consider a point charge +q located in free space. Imagine a spherical Gaussian surface of radius r centered on charge q.
  2. By spherical symmetry, the magnitude of the electric field E is identical at all points on the surface, pointing radially outward everywhere.
  3. Therefore, the angle θ between E and the area vector dA is 0° (cos 0° = 1).
  4. Evaluate the surface integral:
    ∮ E · dA = ∮ E dA = E ∮ dA
  5. Since the total area of a sphere of radius r is A = 4πr2:

  6. Placing a test charge q0 at distance r, the electrostatic force experienced is F = q0E:
    F =

This explicitly derives Coulomb's Law.

2. Heat & Thermodynamics

Mean Free Path (λ)

The average distance traveled by a gas molecule between successive collisions.

λ =
1 √2 · π · d2 · n

Where d is molecular diameter and n = N / V is molecular number density.

Dependence on Temperature and Pressure: Using Ideal Gas Law (P = n kB T):

λ =
kB · T √2 · π · d2 · P
  • At constant pressure, mean free path increases linearly with Temperature (λ ∝ T).
  • At constant temperature, mean free path is inversely proportional to Pressure (λ ∝ 1/P).
First Law of Thermodynamics

A statement of energy conservation applied to thermodynamic systems.

dQ = dU + dW   ⇒   dQ = dU + P dV
  • dQ: Heat transferred to the system (+ if absorbed, - if released).
  • dU: Change in internal energy (+ if temperature rises).
  • dW: Work done by the system (+ for expansion, - for compression).
Second Law of Thermodynamics (Classical Statements)
  • Kelvin-Planck Statement: No process is possible whose sole result is the absorption of heat from a single reservoir and its complete conversion into work (no 100% efficient heat engine).
  • Clausius Statement: No process is possible whose sole result is the transfer of heat from a cooler body to a hotter body without external work input.
  • Entropy Statement: In any irreversible spontaneous process, the total entropy of the universe always increases (ΔSuniv > 0).
Reversible vs. Irreversible Processes
Parameter Reversible Process Irreversible Process

Isothermal & Adiabatic Processes

Isothermal Process (ΔT = 0): System remains at constant temperature by exchanging heat with surroundings.

  • Equation: P · V = Constant
  • Internal energy change: dU = 0  ⇒  dQ = dW
  • Work done: Wiso = n · R · T · ln(V2 / V1)

Adiabatic Process (dQ = 0): System is thermally insulated; no heat enters or leaves.

  • Equation: P · Vγ = Constant (where γ = Cp / Cv)
  • First Law relation: dU = -dW
  • Work done: Wadia =
    P1V1 - P2V2 γ - 1
    =
    n R (T1 - T2) γ - 1

Carnot Engine & Carnot's Theorem

The Carnot cycle is an ideal reversible cycle operating between hot source TH and cold sink TC consisting of 4 steps:

  1. Reversible Isothermal Expansion at temperature TH (Absorbs QH).
  2. Reversible Adiabatic Expansion (Temperature drops from TH to TC).
  3. Reversible Isothermal Compression at temperature TC (Rejects QC).
  4. Reversible Adiabatic Compression (Temperature rises from TC to TH).
Thermal Efficiency: η =
W QH
=
QH - QC QH
= 1 -
QC QH
= 1 -
TC TH

Carnot's Theorem:

  1. No real engine operating between two heat reservoirs can be more efficient than a Carnot engine operating between the same reservoirs.
  2. All reversible engines operating between the same two temperatures have identical efficiency regardless of working substance.
Entropy Concept (S)

Entropy is a state function measuring microscopic disorder or unavailable energy in a thermodynamic system.

dS =
dQrev T

For a complete reversible cycle: ∮ (dQrev / T) = 0. For an irreversible cycle (Clausius Inequality): ∮ (dQ / T) < 0.
