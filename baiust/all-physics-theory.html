Gauss's Law & Coulomb's Law Derivation

Gauss's Law Statement: The net electric flux ΦE passing through any imaginary closed surface (Gaussian surface) is equal to the total net enclosed electric charge Qenc divided by the vacuum permittivity ε0.

Step-by-Step Derivation of Coulomb's Law from Gauss's Law:

  1. Consider an isolated stationary point charge +q in vacuum. Enclose it within a concentric spherical Gaussian surface of radius r.
  2. Due to spherical symmetry, the electric field vector E has uniform magnitude at all surface points and points radially outwards.
  3. The normal area vector dA also points radially outward. Thus, the angle θ between E and dA is 0° (cos 0° = 1).
  4. Evaluate the closed surface integral:

Capacitance of a Parallel Plate Capacitor

A parallel plate capacitor consists of two parallel conducting plates of area A separated by a small gap d.

  1. Charges +Q and -Q create uniform surface charge density σ = Q / A.
  2. Between the plates, electric field contributions add up: E = σ / ε0 = Q / (ε0 A).
  3. Potential difference across gap d is V = E · d = (Q · d) / (ε0 A).

  where Magnetic Flux ΦB = ∬ B · dA = B · A · cos(θ)

Lenz's Law Statement: The direction of an induced current is always such that its magnetic field opposes the original change in magnetic flux that induced it.

Energy Conservation Proof: If induced current reinforced the flux change instead of opposing it, continuous mechanical work could be extracted without input energy, violating energy conservation. The negative sign ensures mechanical work spent overcoming magnetic repulsion converts directly into electrical energy.

2. Heat & Thermodynamics

Mean Free Path (λ)

The average distance traveled by a gas molecule between successive collisions with other gas molecules.

λ =
1 √2 · π · d2 · n

Where d is molecular collision diameter and n = N / V is number of molecules per unit volume.

Dependence on Temperature and Pressure: Substituting n = P / (kB T) from the ideal gas law:

λ =
kB · T √2 · π · d2 · P
  • At constant pressure P, mean free path increases linearly with absolute temperature T (λ ∝ T).
  • At constant temperature T, mean free path is inversely proportional to pressure P (λ ∝ 1/P).
First Law of Thermodynamics & Specific Processes

A statement of energy conservation stating that net heat supplied to a system equals internal energy change plus work done.

dQ = dU + dW   ⇒   dQ = dU + P dV

Application to Specific Processes:

  • Isochoric Process (Constant Volume, dV = 0): dW = 0  ⇒  dQ = dU = n Cv dT
  • Isobaric Process (Constant Pressure, dP = 0): dQ = n Cp dT,  dW = P(V2 - V1)
  • Isothermal Process (Constant Temp, dT = 0): dU = 0  ⇒  dQ = dW
  • Adiabatic Process (Insulated, dQ = 0): dU = -dW
Second Law of Thermodynamics (Equivalences)
  • Kelvin-Planck Statement: It is impossible for any device operating on a cycle to receive heat from a single thermal reservoir and produce a net amount of work (100% efficient heat engine is impossible).
  • Clausius Statement: It is impossible to construct a cyclic device that operates such that heat is transferred from a low-temperature body to a high-temperature body without external work input (spontaneous heat flow is strictly unidirectional from hot to cold).
  • Clausius Inequality: For any arbitrary thermodynamic cycle:

Carnot Engine & Carnot's Theorem

The Carnot Engine is a theoretical reversible heat engine operating between hot reservoir TH and cold reservoir TC in four steps:

  1. Reversible Isothermal Expansion (1 → 2): System absorbs heat QH at temperature TH.
  2. Reversible Adiabatic Expansion (2 → 3): Working fluid cools from TH to TC doing mechanical work.
  3. Reversible Isothermal Compression (3 → 4): System rejects heat QC at temperature TC.
  4. Reversible Adiabatic Compression (4 → 1): Fluid compresses, raising temperature back from TC to TH.

Carnot's Theorem: No real engine operating between two given temperatures can be more efficient than a Carnot engine operating between the exact same temperatures.
