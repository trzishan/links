1. Background & Context
  • Core Definition: A historic charter of demands enunciated by the Awami League to eliminate regional economic disparity and terminate the internal colonial rule of West Pakistan in East Bengal [cite: 2].
  • Catalyst Events:
    • Exacerbated economic disparities between East and West Pakistan [cite: 2].
    • The 1965 Indo-Pak War and subsequent Tashkent Treaty exposed central government negligence and total indifference regarding East Pakistan's defence [cite: 2].

3. The Six Demands (Core Points)
  • Point 1: True Federation & Parliamentary System → Federal government based on the Lahore Resolution; parliamentary form with legislature elected directly via universal adult franchise [cite: 2].
  • Point 2: Restricted Central Powers → Federal center handles only Defence and Foreign Affairs; all other residuary powers vested in federating states [cite: 2].
  • Point 3: Currency & Monetary Control → Two freely convertible currencies for the two wings, OR one currency with constitutional checks to prevent capital flight from East to West; establishment of a separate Banking Reserve & fiscal policy for East Pakistan [cite: 2].
  • Point 4: Taxation Autonomy → Power of taxation and revenue collection vested strictly in federating units; center receives a shared percentage to cover expenditures [cite: 2].
  • Point 5: Foreign Trade & Earnings → Separate foreign exchange accounts for both wings; federal requirements shared equally; free duty-free movement of indigenous goods; units empowered to establish direct foreign trade links [cite: 2].
  • Point 6: Regional Militia → Provision for East Pakistan to maintain its own separate militia or paramilitary force [cite: 2].
4. Suppression, Resistance & Political Impact
  • State Backlash: The Ayub Khan administration rejected the demands, labeled Mujib a separatist, and filed the fabricated Agartala Conspiracy Case, arresting Mujib and putting him on trial [cite: 2].
  • 1969 Mass Uprising: Prosecution sparked widespread public rebellion across East Pakistan, forcing the government to release Sheikh Mujib unconditionally on 22 February 1969 [cite: 2].
  • 1970 General Elections Mandate: Awami League contested the general elections with the Six-Point Programme as its manifesto, securing an absolute mandate from the people of East Pakistan [cite: 2].
  • The Collapse of Negotiations: Zulfiqar Ali Bhutto boycotted the National Assembly session scheduled for March 3, 1971 [cite: 2]. Protracted talks between Mujib and West Pakistani leaders from March 15 failed, leading to the March 25 army crackdown that ended the fate of the Six-Point and unified Pakistan [cite: 2].
