HISTORICAL BRIEFING • 1971 LIBERATION WAR

OPERATION SEARCHLIGHT

An Analysis & Key Highlights of the March 25, 1971 Military Crackdown

7,000+ Killed (Dhaka Night 1)
11:30 PM Actual Launch (March 25)
8 Zones Initial Targets
1. Executive Overview & Strategic Objectives
  • Definition: Operation Searchlight was a brutal, systematic military operation launched by the Pakistan Armed Forces on 25 March 1971 to crush the Bengali self-determination movement and non-cooperation campaign led by Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman.
  • Core Objective: Neutralize political resistance, neutralize Bengali leadership, and regain total control over East Pakistan.
  • Primary Target Groups: Prominent Awami League leaders, student leaders, Bengali intellectuals, university professors, and Bengali personnel in military, paramilitary (EPR), and police forces.
  • Key Infrastructure Targets: Armouries, radio stations, telephone exchanges, and media offices.
2. Timeline Shift & Pre-emption
  • Original Schedule: Official launch was planned for 13:00 hrs on 26 March 1971.
  • The Trigger: Talks between Sheikh Mujib and Yahya Khan failed on March 25. Mujib called for an all-out struggle, leading civilians to build street barricades across Dhaka.
  • Pre-emptive Strike: Military leadership brought the operation forward by 13.5 hours to 11:30 PM on 25 March to catch resistance off guard.
3. Command Structure
  • Maj. Gen. Khadim Hossain Raja & Maj. Gen. Rao Farman Ali Khan: Initiated decision on 22 February 1971. Rao Farman Ali authored the 5-page plan on 18 March.
  • Operational Leadership: Dhaka expedition led by Maj. Gen. Rao Farman Ali; regional operations led by Maj. Gen. Khadim Hossain Raja; overall supervision by Lt. Gen. Tikka Khan.
4. Tactical Unit Assignments (Dhaka Focus)
  • 22-Baluch Regiment → Disarm 5,000 Bengali EPR troops at Peelkhana; capture radio center.
  • 32-Punjab Regiment → Disarm 1,000 armed Bengali policemen at Rajarbagh Police Lines.
  • 18-Punjab Regiment → Assault Hindu-majority areas in Nawabpur and Old Dhaka.
  • SSG Commando Platoon → Attack Dhanmondi residence and arrest Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman (arrested at 1:30 AM).
  • Joint Unit Assaults → Attack Dhaka University student halls (Iqbal, Jagannath, Rokeya) and BUET (Liaquat Hall), killing students and 9 university professors.

HISTORICAL CHARTER • 1966 LIBERATION MOVEMENT

SIX-POINT PROGRAMME

The Charter of Demands & Autonomy Movement of East Bengal

Feb 1966 First Unveiled (Lahore)
1970 Mandate Landslide Victory
22 Feb 1969 Mujib Released
1. Background & Context
  • Core Definition: A charter enunciated by the Awami League to remove regional economic disparity and terminate West Pakistan's internal colonial rule[cite: 2].
  • Catalyst: The 1965 Indo-Pak War and Taskent Treaty exposed the central government's total negligence toward East Pakistan's defense[cite: 2].
2. The Six Demands
  • Point 1: True Federation → Parliamentary government based on the Lahore Resolution with direct adult franchise[cite: 2].
  • Point 2: Restricted Central Powers → Federal center handles only Defence and Foreign Affairs; all other subjects vested in states[cite: 2].
  • Point 3: Currency & Banking → Two separate freely convertible currencies OR constitutional checks against capital flight from East to West; separate Banking Reserve for East Pakistan[cite: 2].
  • Point 4: Taxation Autonomy → Power of taxation vested strictly in federating units; center receives a shared percentage[cite: 2].
  • Point 5: Foreign Trade → Separate foreign exchange accounts; freedom to establish direct international trade links[cite: 2].
  • Point 6: Regional Militia → Provision for East Pakistan to maintain a separate militia/paramilitary force[cite: 2].
3. Suppression & Impact
  • Agartala Conspiracy Case: Ayub regime jailed Mujib under treason charges, sparking the 1969 Mass Uprising which forced his unconditional release on 22 Feb 1969[cite: 2].
  • 1970 Electoral Mandate: Awami League won an absolute public mandate on the Six-Point manifesto[cite: 2].

HISTORICAL BRIEFING • 1969 MASS MOVEMENT

MASS UPSURGE, 1969

The Great Public Awakening That Overthrew the Ayub Regime

4 Jan 1969 11-Point Formed
22 Feb 1969 Mujib Released
25 Mar 1969 Ayub Resigns
1. Escalation & Key Martyrs
  • 20 Jan 1969: Asaduzzaman (Students Union Menon Group) shot dead by police during a DU procession[cite: 1].
  • 24 Jan 1969: Student Matiur and Rustam killed by police firing; indefinite curfew imposed[cite: 1].
  • 15 Feb 1969: Custodial murder of Sergeant Zahrul Huq (Agartala case undertrial) in Dhaka Cantonment[cite: 1].
  • 18 Feb 1969: Dr. Mohammad Shamsuzzoha (Proctor, Rajshahi University) bayoneted to death by military forces[cite: 1].
2. Victory & Regime Collapse
  • Case Withdrawal: Ayub Khan withdrew the Agartala Conspiracy Case and released Sheikh Mujib unconditionally on 22 Feb 1969[cite: 1].
  • Resignation: On 25 March 1969, Ayub Khan resigned and handed power to General Yahya Khan, who agreed to hold future elections based on universal adult franchise[cite: 1].

HISTORICAL CHARTER • 1969 STUDENT MOVEMENT

ELEVEN POINTS PROGRAMME

The Unified Charter of Demands of Sarbadaliya Chhatra Sangram Parishad

Summary of All 11 Demands
  • 1. Education → Repeal Hamidur Rahman Commission report & DU Ordinance; Bangla as medium of instruction.
  • 2. Democracy → Parliamentary form via adult franchise; restore press freedom.
  • 3. Autonomy → Full provincial autonomy based on Awami League's Six-Point Programme.
  • 4. Dissolve One Unit → Restructure West Pakistan into autonomous provinces (Punjab, Sindh, KPK, Balochistan).
  • 5. Nationalization → Nationalize banks, insurance companies, and raw jute mills.
  • 6. Peasant Relief → Lower agricultural taxes; abolish Tahsildar certificate system; fix minimum jute price at Taka 40/maund.
  • 7. Labor Protection → Fair wages, medical care, housing, and right to strike.
  • 8. Water Control → Implement comprehensive flood control in East Pakistan.
  • 9. Law Repeal → Immediately withdraw Emergency Act and Public Safety Act.
  • 10. Foreign Policy → Cancel Pak-US military pacts; exit SEATO/CENTO.
  • 11. Release Detainees → Unconditionally release political prisoners; drop Agartala Conspiracy Case.

HISTORICAL BRIEFING • 1970 GENERAL ELECTIONS

1970 PAKISTANI GENERAL ELECTION

The Democratic Mandate That Reshaped South Asia

167 / 313 Awami League Total
86 / 313 PPP Total Seats
160 / 162 AL East Pak General
Electoral Results & Crisis
  • Awami League Victory: Won 160 of 162 general seats in East Pakistan (+7 reserved women seats), securing an absolute majority in the National Assembly (167/313).
  • PPP Victory in West: Zulfikar Ali Bhutto's PPP won 86 seats (Punjab and Sindh), but zero in East Pakistan.
  • Political Deadlock: Ruling elites and Bhutto refused to transfer power to Mujib, postponing the March 3 assembly session, which sparked non-cooperation and the Liberation War.

HISTORICAL BRIEFING • 1971 LIBERATION WAR

GENOCIDE, 1971

The Systematic Mass Atrocities in East Pakistan

~3 Million Fatalities
10 Million Refugees to India
267 Days 25 Mar – 16 Dec 1971
Key Elements of Atrocities
  • Systematic Massacres: Launched under Operation Searchlight on 25 March 1971, killing over 50,000 civilians in the first 3 days across major towns.
  • Collaborator Militias: Formation of Razakar, Al-Badr, and Al-Shams forces to terrorize communities and abduct targets.
  • Decapitation of Intellectuals: Al-Badr/Al-Shams forces executed a targeted plan to systematically abduct and kill top doctors, professors, journalists, and writers before surrender.
