HISTORICAL CHARTER • 1969 STUDENT MOVEMENT

ELEVEN POINTS PROGRAMME

The Unified Charter of Demands of Sarbadaliya Chhatra Sangram Parishad

4 Jan 1969 SAC Formally Formed 11 Points Comprehensive Charter 1969 Uprising Catalyst for Ayub's Fall
1. Background & Formation
  • Core Definition: A historic charter of demands framed by student leaders to remedy acute economic disparity between East and West Pakistan, expand political freedom, and overthrow the autocratic regime of Ayub Khan [cite: 1.1.1].
  • Political Context:
    • The Six-Point Movement suffered a temporary setback due to mass arrests of Awami League leaders and the filing of the Agartala Conspiracy Case in 1968 [cite: 1.1.1].
    • On 4 January 1969, major student bodies formed the Sarbadaliya Chhatra Sangram Parishad (All-Party Student Action Committee - SAC) led by DUCSU Vice President Tofael Ahmed alongside leaders from East Pakistan Students League and Students Union factions (Menon & Matia groups) [cite: 1.1.3, 1.1.4, 1.1.6].
2. Complete Breakdown of the 11 Points
  • Point 1: Educational Reforms & Bangla Language → Repeal anti-Bengali educational policies (e.g., Hamidur Rahman Education Commission report & DU Ordinance); establish schools/colleges in rural areas; make Bangla the medium of instruction and court/office exchange; lower tuition fees [cite: 1.1.1, 1.1.3].
  • Point 2: Parliamentary Democracy → Restore parliamentary government elected via universal adult franchise; guarantee press freedom and remove bans on newspapers like The Daily Ittefaq [cite: 1.1.3].
  • Point 3: Provincial Autonomy → Grant full autonomy to East Pakistan based on the Awami League's Six-Point Programme [cite: 1.1.1, 1.1.3, 1.1.6].
  • Point 4: Federation & Dissolution of "One Unit" → Dissolve the "One Unit" scheme in West Pakistan, restructuring it into autonomous provinces (Punjab, Sindh, KPK, Balochistan) [cite: 1.1.3].
  • Point 5: Nationalization of Key Industries → Nationalize banks, insurance companies, and large industrial units, including raw jute processing plants [cite: 1.1.3].
  • Point 6: Peasant Rights & Land Tax Relief → Reduce land revenue/taxes on farmers, exempt arrear debts, abolish the oppressive Tahsildar certificate system, and guarantee raw jute minimum price at Taka 40 per maund [cite: 1.1.1, 1.1.3].
  • Point 7: Labor & Worker Protection → Guarantee fair wages, bonuses, medical care, housing, and education for workers; repeal anti-labor "black laws" and protect right to strike and trade unions [cite: 1.1.1, 1.1.3].
  • Point 8: Flood Control & Water Resources → Implement comprehensive flood control projects and ensure optimal utilization of water resources in East Pakistan [cite: 1.1.1, 1.1.3].
  • Point 9: Abrogation of Repressive Acts → Immediately withdraw Emergency Act, Public Safety Act, and all draconian laws enacted by Ayub Khan's regime [cite: 1.1.1, 1.1.3].
  • Point 10: Independent & Non-Aligned Foreign Policy → Cancel Pak-US military pacts, exit SEATO and CENTO alliances, and adopt an independent, neutral foreign policy [cite: 1.1.1, 1.1.3].
  • Point 11: Release of Prisoners & Agartala Withdrawal → Unconditionally release all political prisoners, students, workers, and peasants; withdraw all warrants and drop cases, including the Agartala Conspiracy Case [cite: 1.1.1, 1.1.3].
3. Historical Impact & Legacy
  • Unifying Opposition: Combined student, labor, peasant, and political interests under a single unified platform, rallying wide public support [cite: 1.1.1, 1.1.6].
  • Leadership Pivot: Shifted the vanguard of the anti-Ayub movement directly into the hands of student leaders [cite: 1.1.1, 1.1.3].
  • Engine of the 1969 Mass Uprising: Triggered street protests culminating in the release of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman and the ultimate resignation of President Ayub Khan in March 1969 [cite: 1.1.1, 1.1.3, 1.1.6].
