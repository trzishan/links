HISTORICAL BRIEFING • 1971 LIBERATION WAR

OPERATION SEARCHLIGHT

An Analysis & Key Highlights of the March 25, 1971 Military Crackdown

7,000+ Killed (Dhaka Night 1) 11:30 PM Actual Launch (March 25) 8 Zones Initial Targets
1. Executive Overview & Strategic Objectives
  • Definition: Operation Searchlight was a brutal, systematic military operation launched by the Pakistan Armed Forces on 25 March 1971 to crush the Bengali self-determination movement and non-cooperation campaign led by Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman.
  • Core Objective: Neutralize political resistance, neutralize Bengali leadership, and regain total control over East Pakistan.
  • Primary Target Groups:
    • Prominent Awami League political leaders and student leaders
    • Bengali intellectuals, university professors, and cultural activists
    • Bengali personnel serving in military, paramilitary (EPR), and police forces
  • Key Infrastructure Targets: Armouries, radio stations, telephone exchanges, and media offices.
  • Timeframe: Commenced 25 March 1971 at 11:30 PM; major operations continued through mid-May 1971 across all major urban centers.
2. Timeline Shift & Tactical Pre-emption
  • Original Schedule: Official launch was planned for 13:00 hrs on 26 March 1971.
  • The Trigger: On the evening of 25 March, Sheikh Mujibur Rahman called for an all-out struggle after talks with President Yahya Khan yielded no tangible results. Bengali civilians quickly raised street barricades across Dhaka.
  • Pre-emptive Execution: To catch resistance unprepared, military leadership brought the operation forward by 13.5 hours to 11:30 PM on 25 March (noted by PRO Major Siddique Salek).
  • Pakistan's Justification: A White Paper published by Pakistan (5 August 1971) claimed the schedule was moved forward because the Awami League planned an armed revolution at dawn on 26 March.
3. Command Structure & Masterminds
  • Maj. Gen. Khadim Hossain Raja (GOC, 14th Division) & Maj. Gen. Rao Farman Ali Khan (GOC, 57th Division): Initiated operational decision on 22 February 1971.
  • Rao Farman Ali: Personally authored the official 5-page operational plan on 18 March at Dhaka Cantonment.
  • General Abdul Hamid Khan (Chief of Staff): Ordered finalization of plans on 17 March; conducted helicopter inspections of regional cantonments with Col. Sadullah & Gen. A.O. Miththi on 24–25 March.
  • Operational Leadership Split:
    • Dhaka Expedition: Led by Maj. Gen. Rao Farman Ali.
    • Regional Operations: Led by Maj. Gen. Khadim Hossain Raja.
    • Overall Supervision: Lt. Gen. Tikka Khan from 31 Field Command post.
  • Presidential Emissaries: Maj. Gen. Iftekhar Janjua and Maj. Gen. A.O. Miththi summoned directly by Yahya Khan to assist.
4. Eight-Point Operational Strategy
  • Simultaneous Execution: Launch attacks simultaneously across all of East Pakistan.
  • Mass Arrests: Round up politicians, student leaders, university faculty, and extremist cultural activists.
  • 100% Dhaka Control: Total military control of Dhaka, prioritizing the capture of Dhaka University.
  • Base Security: Ensure total security of military cantonments.
  • Communication Blackout: Sever all domestic/international comms (telephone, radio, TV, teleprinters, foreign consulate transmitters).
  • Disarm Bengali Troops: Disarm East Pakistan Rifles (EPR) soldiers; replace them with West Pakistani troops at armouries.
  • Phase 1 Operational Zones: Dhaka, Chittagong, Comilla, Jessore, Khulna, Rangpur, Syedpur, Sylhet (air support authorized).
  • Eliminate Barricades: Clear all road blockades and assault listed political residences.
5. Tactical Unit Assignments (Dhaka Focus)
  • 22-Baluch Regiment → Neutralize & disarm 5,000 Bengali EPR troops at Peelkhana; capture radio center.
  • 32-Punjab Regiment → Disarm 1,000 armed Bengali policemen at Rajarbagh Police Lines.
  • 18-Punjab Regiment → Assault Hindu-majority areas in Nawabpur and Old Dhaka.
  • Joint Force (22-Baluch, 18/32 Punjab) → Storm Dhaka University halls (Iqbal, Jagannath) & BUET (Liaquat Hall).
  • Special Service Group (SSG) Platoon → Assault Dhanmondi residence and arrest Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman (arrested at 1:30 AM).
  • Field Regiment → Secure Second Capital and adjoining areas (Mirpur & Mohammadpur).
  • Armored Unit → Deploy M24 light tank squadrons on major roads for intimidation and shelling.
6. Execution, Casualties & Atrocities
  • Initial Clash: First shots fired at Farmgate at 11:30 PM against demonstrating Bengali civilians clearing barricades.
  • Academic Massacre: Night raids on Dhaka University residence halls (Iqbal, Jagannath, Rokeya) and faculty quarters resulted in mass slaughters of students and 9 university professors.
  • City-Wide Assaults: Heavy shelling/attacks in Tejgaon, Indira Road, Mirpur, Mohammadpur, Dhaka Airport, Ganaktuli, Dhanmondi, Kalabagan, Kathalbagan, and Chittagong.
  • Press Suppression: Offices of pro-liberation newspapers (The Daily Ittefaq, Sangbad, The People) torched; numerous journalists burned alive inside.
  • Estimated Body Count:
    • 7,000 Bengalis killed on night 1 in Dhaka city alone.
    • 700 people burned to death in Old Dhaka.
    • 200+ students killed at Iqbal Hall alone (reported by Simon Dring).
7. Foreign Press Reporting & Political Reaction
  • Press Censorship & Expulsion: Most foreign correspondents were forcibly deported prior to March 25.
  • Heroic Journalism: Simon Dring (Daily Telegraph), Arnold Zeitlin, and Michel Laurent evaded capture in Dhaka to report the initial massacres ("Dateline Dacca").
  • Political Responses:
    • President Yahya Khan: Secretly fled Dhaka for Karachi right before operations commenced.
    • Zulfiqar Ali Bhutto: Observed the night assault from Hotel Intercontinental; famously stated, "Thanks to God that Pakistan could have been saved."
    • Pakistani State Narrative: Officially classified the military operation as "inevitable" in its August 5 White Paper.
