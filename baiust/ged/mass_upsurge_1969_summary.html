MASS UPSURGE, 1969

The Great Public Awakening That Overthrew the Ayub Regime

4 Jan 1969 11-Point Programme Formed 22 Feb 1969 Mujib Released Unconditionally 25 Mar 1969 Ayub Khan Resigns Power
1. Executive Overview & Root Causes
  • Core Definition: The 1969 Mass Upsurge was the greatest mass awakening in East Pakistan since the 1947 creation of Pakistan, uniting students, workers, peasants, and political parties against President Ayub Khan's autocratic rule [cite: 1].
  • Underlying Causes:
    • Racial Repression & Deprivation: Systematic political and economic discrimination against Bengalis within Pakistan [cite: 1].
    • Identity & Autonomy: Evolution of Bengali national identity rooted in the Language Movement and the push for provincial autonomy [cite: 1].
    • Economic Struggle: Low and middle-income groups turned student protests into a struggle for economic emancipation [cite: 1].
2. Initial Mobilization (Late 1968 Timeline)
  • Late 1968 Unrest: Initiated with student unrest against Ayub Khan's regime [cite: 1].
  • 6 December 1968 (Repression Resistance Day):
    • Maulana Abdul Hamid Khan Bhasani (NAP), Toaha (Workers' Federation), and Abdul Huq (Peasants' Association) held a rally at Paltan Maidan [cite: 1].
    • Protesters launched a massive "Gherao" (siege) of the Governor's House following clashes with police [cite: 1].
  • 7–8 December 1968: Nationwide Hartal called by main opposition parties (NAP Bhasani & Muzaffar, Awami League, People's Party, Nezam-i-Islam, Jama't-i-Islami) [cite: 1].
  • 14 & 29 December 1968: Bhasani declared the continuation of the Gherao campaign, kicking off with the siege of the Pabna DC's bungalow on Dec 29 [cite: 1].
3. Alliances & Charters (January 1969)
  • 4 January 1969 — Students' Action Committee (SAC):
    • Formed by Students League, Students Union (Menon & Matia groups), and a faction of the NSF alongside DUCSU [cite: 1].
    • Formulated the 11-Point Programme, incorporating Awami League's 6 Points, student autonomy demands, and labor rights [cite: 1].
  • 8 January 1969 — Democratic Action Committee (DAC):
    • Formed by 8 opposition political parties (including Awami League and NAP Muzaffar) [cite: 1].
    • Demanded federal government, universal adult franchise, emergency rule withdrawal, and release of all political detainees (Sheikh Mujib, Wali Khan, Zulfiqar Ali Bhutto) [cite: 1].
4. Key Martyrs & Escalation Phase
  • 20 January 1969 — Martyrdom of Asaduzzaman: Asad, a leader of the Students Union (Menon), was shot dead by police during a DU campus procession, turning the agitation into a full national upsurge [cite: 1].
  • 24 January 1969 — Mass Murders & Curfew: Class IX student Matiur and Rustam were killed by police fire; army deployed and indefinite curfew imposed after indiscriminate firing killed civilians [cite: 1].
  • 15 February 1969 — Custodial Death of Sergeant Zahrul Huq: An undertrial prisoner in the Agartala Conspiracy Case, Zahrul Huq was shot in Dhaka Cantonment [cite: 1]. Bhasani threatened a French Revolution-style Bastille storming to free Mujib, sparking widespread arson of ministers' homes [cite: 1].
  • 18 February 1969 — Killing of Dr. Shamsuzzoha: Dr. Mohammad Shamsuzzoha, Proctor of Rajshahi University, was bayoneted to death by troops, prompting massive street defiance of curfews nationwide [cite: 1].
5. Radical Popular Actions & Mass Uprising
  • Rural Peasant Uprising: Peasants and students set up local tribunals, tried corrupt tax collectors, police, and circle officers, forcing union council chairmen to resign [cite: 1].
  • Urban Worker Gheraos: Industrial workers utilized the Gherao movement to paralyze factories and secure long-denied economic rights [cite: 1].
  • Demolishing Bureaucracy: Breakdown of state authority minimized popular fear of police, civil, and military bureaucrats [cite: 1].
6. Victory, Regime Collapse & Long-Term Impact
  • Collapse of Agartala Case: Unable to control the territory, Ayub Khan withdrew the Agartala Conspiracy Case and announced he would not contest the next Presidential election [cite: 1].
  • 22 February 1969: Sheikh Mujibur Rahman and 34 co-accused were released unconditionally to a hero's welcome [cite: 1].
  • Fallback to Round Table Talks: Sheikh Mujib joined Ayub's Round Table Conference (RTC) appealing for peace, while Bhasani boycotted it, framing the movement as an irreconcilable struggle between oppressors and the oppressed [cite: 1].
  • Fall of Ayub Khan (25 March 1969): Ayub resigned and handed power to Army Chief Gen. Yahya Khan [cite: 1]. Yahya re-imposed Martial Law but agreed to hold elections based on universal adult franchise [cite: 1].
  • Historical Significance: Solidified Bengali nationalism, built class consciousness, destroyed bureaucratic fear, and set the direct trajectory toward the 1971 War of Liberation [cite: 1].
